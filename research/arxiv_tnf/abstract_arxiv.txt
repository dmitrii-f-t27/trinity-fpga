A ternary node has three sites that could carry a number format; one needs to. The weight is a code: multiplying by it is a sign-select. The sample arrives ADC-native. The accumulator alone has a range to spend, and Ternary Network Floats spend it -- a sign, a balanced-ternary exponent of Et trits, an M-bit mantissa, 1+Et+M=N. The weight is GFTernary, a two-bit \-phi,0,+phi\ alphabet, and it is where the multiplier is decided. Removing one rather than cheapening it requires the weight lattice to be closed under weight application and accumulation alike. Z[phi] is, because phi^2=phi+1: applying a weight is the Fibonacci step (a,b)->(b,a+b), accumulation is componentwise, and a layer's linear path is exact. Machine-checked in Coq. A set that is not closed must return every accumulation to it, costing an order of magnitude more area, and pipelining does not rescue it. Closure does not single out phi; enumeration does. A scale is multiplier-free exactly when it is an algebraic integer whose companion matrix has entries in \0,1\ -- a finite condition. Degree two admits phi alone, and nothing lies between the shift and phi. The family r^d=r+1 has two non-zero coefficients at every degree, so any granularity is reachable at one addition and d registers; fineness costs registers, not adders. On an XC7A200T in a fully open flow a ternary neuron costs 28 LUT per weight, 32.4 with one pipeline stage at 126.73 MHz, and DSP at any fan-in, as does the stack above it. Which rung follows from the budget -- phi at four bits, r^5=r^3+1 at five, r^6=r+1 at six -- reaching 3.7% of fp32 at six bits and one adder. On the block axis the element is closed, the best eight-level codebook beating a deployed one by 0.9%; the scale is not, a phi grid at four bits beating MXFP4 on two models at 0.125 fewer bits per weight.
